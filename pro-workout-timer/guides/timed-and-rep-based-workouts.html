Workout timer guide

How to time a workout that mixes holds, reps, and rest

Many workout timers assume that every exercise ends when a countdown reaches zero.

That works for a 30-second plank, but not for 12 push-ups, five pull-ups, or a set that should finish when your technique—not the clock—says it is done.

The usual workaround is to estimate how long every rep-based exercise will take.

That makes the timer easier to configure, but it also makes the workout rush you when a set takes longer and leave dead time when you finish early.

Use two different step types

A better structure is to treat timed and rep-based exercises as two different step types.

  • A timed step advances automatically after its countdown.
  • A rep-based step waits until you confirm that the prescribed reps are complete.
  • Rest starts only after the current step actually finishes.
  • Sets and circuits repeat independently of the individual exercise type.

A mixed circuit example

  1. Plank — 30 seconds.
  2. Push-ups — 12 reps.
  3. Kettlebell swings — 15 reps.
  4. Rest — 45 seconds.
  5. Repeat the circuit three times.

The timed hold stays precise, while the strength movements adapt to your real pace.

You also avoid switching between a timer, a notes app, and your memory just to track the next transition.

What to check before choosing a timer

  • Can timed and rep-based steps exist in the same saved workout?
  • Can a rep-based step wait for manual completion instead of guessing a duration?
  • Can a complete set repeat with its own rest period?
  • Will audio cues or notifications continue if the phone is locked or another app is open?
